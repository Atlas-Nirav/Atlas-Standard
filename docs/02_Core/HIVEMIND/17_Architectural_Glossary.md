# 1. Purpose

This glossary defines the canonical architectural terminology used throughout the Atlas Hivemind Specification.

The purpose of this glossary is to ensure consistent interpretation of architectural concepts across specifications, implementations, and engineering disciplines.

Where terminology conflicts arise, the definitions contained in this glossary take precedence.

---

# 2. Architectural Terms

## Artificial Intelligence (AI)

A collection of computational techniques that may support engineering cognition.

Artificial Intelligence is an implementation technology.

It is not the Hivemind.

---

## Architectural Constraint

A mandatory limitation governing the operation of the Hivemind.

Constraints establish architectural boundaries while preserving system integrity.

Defined in HS-011.

---

## Architectural Invariant

A permanent architectural truth that shall always remain valid.

Invariants define the constitutional laws of the Hivemind.

Defined in HS-010.

---

## Capability

A specialized engineering discipline or reasoning function that contributes knowledge and analysis to the Hivemind.

Capabilities contribute toward Engineering Understanding but do not own it.

---

## Cognition

The continuous process of observing, interpreting, reasoning, learning, and improving Engineering Understanding.

---

## Cognitive Cycle

The recurring sequence through which the Hivemind transforms engineering evidence into Engineering Understanding.

Defined in HS-007.

---

## Confidence

An explicit assessment of the reliability and completeness of an engineering conclusion.

Confidence represents uncertainty.

It does not represent correctness.

---

## Context

The information required to correctly interpret engineering evidence.

Context may include spatial, temporal, functional, regulatory, organizational, or lifecycle information.

---

## Decision Support

The presentation of engineering recommendations, alternatives, trade-offs, confidence, and supporting evidence to qualified engineers.

Decision Support does not replace engineering authority.

---

## Engineering Capability

A discipline-specific contributor of engineering expertise, such as Architecture, Structural Engineering, MEP, Construction, Sustainability, or Cost Engineering.

Engineering Capabilities enhance Engineering Understanding.

---

## Engineering Cognition

The continuous transformation of engineering evidence into Engineering Understanding through observation, interpretation, reasoning, learning, and knowledge evolution.

---

## Engineering Evidence

Validated engineering information supporting Engineering Understanding.

Evidence may originate from design models, calculations, regulations, inspections, sensors, simulations, or human expertise.

---

## Engineering Memory

The persistent preservation of historical engineering knowledge, decisions, lessons learned, and organizational experience.

Engineering Memory enables continuous learning.

---

## Engineering Reality

The actual state of the engineering project, including both physical and digital observations.

Engineering Reality is the source of engineering evidence.

---

## Engineering Understanding

The canonical, continuously evolving cognitive representation of an engineering project.

Engineering Understanding integrates evidence, context, intent, relationships, reasoning, constraints, confidence, history, and organizational knowledge.

It is the architectural core of the Hivemind.

Defined in HS-005.

---

## Explainability

The ability to trace every engineering conclusion back to its supporting evidence, reasoning, assumptions, and confidence.

---

## Hivemind

The Engineering Mind of Atlas.

The Hivemind continuously creates, maintains, validates, preserves, and evolves Engineering Understanding.

Defined in HS-002.

---

## Human Authority

The principle that qualified engineers retain final responsibility for engineering decisions.

The Hivemind augments engineering judgment but does not replace it.

---

## Interface

An architectural contract governing the exchange of engineering meaning between the Hivemind and other systems.

Interfaces define responsibilities rather than implementation technologies.

Defined in HS-008.

---

## Knowledge

Validated engineering principles, standards, regulations, methods, and organizational expertise.

Knowledge informs Engineering Understanding but is not equivalent to it.

---

## Non-Goal

A responsibility or behavior intentionally excluded from the scope of the Hivemind.

Defined in HS-012.

---

## Reference Architecture

The canonical architectural organization of the Atlas Hivemind.

Defined in HS-009.

---

## Relationship

An explicit engineering dependency connecting entities, systems, disciplines, constraints, or behaviors.

Relationships are first-class architectural elements.

---

## Reasoning

The process of deriving engineering insight from Engineering Understanding.

Reasoning remains evidence-based and explainable.

---

## Traceability

The ability to reconstruct the complete history of engineering evidence, reasoning, decisions, and understanding.

---

## Understanding

A coherent cognitive comprehension of engineering reality.

Understanding is evidence-based, contextual, explainable, and continuously evolving.

---

## Validation

The process of confirming the authenticity, completeness, consistency, and reliability of engineering evidence before it influences Engineering Understanding.

# 3. Normative Language

The following terms are interpreted according to their normative meaning throughout this specification.

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| SHALL | Mandatory requirement |
| SHALL NOT | Mandatory prohibition |
| SHOULD | Strong recommendation |
| SHOULD NOT | Strong discouragement |
| MAY | Optional capability |

The Architectural Glossary establishes a common language for the Atlas Hivemind.

By providing precise and consistent definitions, it reduces ambiguity, supports interoperability, and ensures that all readers interpret the specification using the same architectural vocabulary.

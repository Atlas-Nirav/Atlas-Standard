# HS-015: Normative Examples

**Specification ID:** HS-015

**Title:** Normative Examples

**Specification:** Atlas Hivemind Specification (AHS)

**Version:** 1.0.0

**Status:** Informative

**Layer:** Reference

**Parent Specification:** Atlas Hivemind

**Depends On:**
- HS-001 through HS-014

---

# 1. Purpose

This specification provides representative examples demonstrating the intended interpretation and application of the Atlas Hivemind Specification.

These examples illustrate how Engineering Understanding is created, maintained, and evolved in accordance with the architectural principles defined by this specification.

Examples support understanding but do not introduce additional normative requirements.

---

# 2. Scope

The examples in this document are illustrative.

They demonstrate conformant behavior under common engineering scenarios.

Implementations are not required to reproduce these examples exactly, provided they satisfy the normative requirements of the Atlas Hivemind Specification.

---

# 3. Example 1 — Architectural Design Revision

## Scenario

An architect modifies the location of a structural wall.

## Engineering Evidence

- Updated BIM model
- Design revision metadata
- Author information
- Timestamp

## Hivemind Behavior

1. Detect the design revision.
2. Validate the incoming evidence.
3. Preserve historical context.
4. Update Engineering Understanding.
5. Identify affected structural systems.
6. Notify relevant Engineering Capabilities.
7. Produce updated engineering insight.
8. Preserve traceability.

## Result

Engineering Understanding evolves while maintaining complete historical continuity.

---

# 4. Example 2 — Building Code Update

## Scenario

A revised fire safety regulation becomes applicable to the project.

## Engineering Evidence

- Updated regulatory publication
- Effective date
- Applicable project scope

## Hivemind Behavior

1. Import the new regulation.
2. Validate its authenticity.
3. Determine affected engineering elements.
4. Evaluate compliance.
5. Generate engineering recommendations.
6. Preserve previous regulatory history.

## Result

Engineering Understanding reflects the updated regulatory environment without losing historical reasoning.

# 5. Example 3 — Structural Conflict

## Scenario

Structural analysis identifies an overloaded beam.

## Engineering Evidence

- Structural calculations
- Load combinations
- Material properties
- Safety factors

## Hivemind Behavior

1. Integrate structural evidence.
2. Update Engineering Understanding.
3. Identify affected relationships.
4. Evaluate downstream impacts.
5. Present alternative engineering solutions.
6. Record confidence levels.

## Result

The Hivemind supports engineering decision-making while preserving explainability.

# 6. Example 4 — Construction Observation

## Scenario

A site inspection identifies a deviation between the design model and the constructed asset.

## Engineering Evidence

- Inspection report
- Site photographs
- Survey measurements

## Hivemind Behavior

1. Record the observation.
2. Validate supporting evidence.
3. Compare against Engineering Understanding.
4. Identify discrepancies.
5. Preserve both planned and observed states.
6. Recommend corrective actions.

## Result

Engineering Understanding accurately reflects both design intent and construction reality.

---

# 7. Example 5 — Operational Feedback

## Scenario

Building sensors report consistently higher energy consumption than predicted.

## Engineering Evidence

- Sensor telemetry
- Energy models
- Environmental conditions
- Occupancy data

## Hivemind Behavior

1. Validate operational data.
2. Compare with expected performance.
3. Identify deviations.
4. Update Engineering Understanding.
5. Generate engineering insights.
6. Record lessons for future projects.

## Result

Operational knowledge contributes to long-term organizational learning.

---

# 8. Example 6 — Human Override

## Scenario

An engineer rejects the Hivemind's preferred recommendation.

## Hivemind Behavior

1. Preserve the recommendation.
2. Record the engineer's decision.
3. Capture supporting rationale.
4. Update Engineering Understanding.
5. Learn from the outcome after implementation.

## Result

Human authority is preserved while organizational knowledge grows.

---

# 9. Example 7 — Conflicting Evidence

## Scenario

Two Engineering Capabilities produce conflicting conclusions.

## Hivemind Behavior

1. Preserve both conclusions.
2. Record supporting evidence.
3. Maintain confidence levels.
4. Avoid premature resolution.
5. Request additional evidence or human review.

## Result

The Hivemind explicitly represents uncertainty rather than creating unsupported certainty.

# 10. Relationship to the Specification

Each example demonstrates one or more normative requirements defined in HS-001 through HS-014.

Examples are intended to clarify architectural intent.

They SHALL NOT override normative requirements.

Where a conflict exists, the normative specification takes precedence.

# 11. Summary

The examples in this specification demonstrate how the Atlas Hivemind behaves in representative engineering scenarios.

They illustrate the practical application of Engineering Cognition, Engineering Understanding, and the governance principles defined throughout the Atlas Hivemind Specification.

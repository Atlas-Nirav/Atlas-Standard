# Purpose

This document defines the Hivemind, the Engineering Mind of Atlas.

The Hivemind is responsible for continuously creating, maintaining, validating, and evolving the Engineering Understanding of every project.

It is the central cognitive system of the Atlas Engineering Cognition Model (AECM).

---

# Definition

The Hivemind is Atlas's shared Engineering Mind.

It continuously maintains Engineering Understanding by integrating evidence, engineering knowledge, context, reasoning, constraints, memory, and learning into one coherent understanding of a project.

The Hivemind does not perform engineering work.

It maintains engineering understanding.

---

# Why the Hivemind Exists

Engineering projects generate enormous amounts of disconnected information.

Drawings.

BIM models.

CAD geometry.

Calculations.

Photographs.

Specifications.

Site observations.

Engineering discussions.

Simulation results.

Codes.

Standards.

Human expertise.

None of these individually represent engineering understanding.

The Hivemind exists to transform fragmented evidence into one continuously evolving Engineering Understanding.

---

# Mission

The Hivemind has one responsibility:

**Maintain the Engineering Understanding of every project.**

Every cognitive function within Atlas ultimately serves this mission.

---

# Engineering Understanding

Engineering Understanding is the canonical representation of every project.

The Hivemind continuously updates this understanding as new evidence becomes available.

Engineering Understanding evolves throughout the lifecycle of a project.

It is never considered complete.

---

# Cognitive Responsibilities

The Hivemind continuously maintains the following cognitive responsibilities.

## Awareness

Maintains awareness of the current project state.

Examples include:

- current edits
- active users
- ongoing simulations
- construction progress
- latest observations
- incoming information

Awareness represents the present.

---

## Understanding

Maintains the current engineering comprehension of the project.

Understanding connects all available evidence into one coherent representation.

---

## Context

Maintains the surrounding engineering circumstances necessary to correctly interpret information.

Context includes project objectives, environment, regulations, stakeholders, and engineering conditions.

---

## Intent

Maintains an understanding of why engineering decisions exist.

Examples include:

- minimize cost
- maximize sustainability
- preserve heritage
- accelerate construction
- improve operational efficiency

Intent influences every recommendation.

---

## Constraints

Maintains engineering limitations affecting the project.

Examples include:

- structural limits
- regulations
- environmental conditions
- site restrictions
- project requirements
- engineering dependencies

---

## Knowledge

Maintains validated engineering principles independent of individual projects.

Knowledge includes:

- engineering standards
- building codes
- scientific principles
- validated engineering practices

---

## Engineering Memory

Maintains accumulated engineering experience.

Engineering Memory preserves:

- decisions
- assumptions
- revisions
- lessons learned
- successful solutions
- engineering failures
- historical reasoning

Knowledge persists beyond projects.

---

## Reasoning

Maintains engineering reasoning about the current project.

Reasoning continuously evaluates engineering consequences as projects evolve.

Reasoning is proactive rather than reactive.

---

## Learning

Maintains continuous improvement of engineering understanding.

Learning incorporates validated engineering knowledge without compromising previously validated understanding.

---

## Decision History

Maintains traceability.

Every engineering recommendation should be explainable through its decision history.

---

## Explainability

Every conclusion maintained by the Hivemind should be explainable.

The Hivemind should always communicate:

- evidence
- assumptions
- reasoning
- confidence
- engineering principles

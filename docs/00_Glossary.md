**Version:** 0.1.0  
**Status:** Active

---

# Purpose

This glossary defines the official terminology used throughout Atlas.

Every architecture document, engineering specification, implementation, and discussion should use these definitions consistently.

When ambiguity exists, the definitions in this document take precedence.

---

# Engineering Intelligence

The collective ability to understand, reason about, preserve, and improve engineering knowledge across the lifecycle of the built environment.

Engineering Intelligence extends beyond information retrieval by continuously applying engineering principles, context, constraints, and reasoning.

---

# Engineering Intelligence Operating System

The complete platform that enables continuous engineering understanding, reasoning, learning, and knowledge preservation throughout the lifecycle of the built environment.

Atlas is an Engineering Intelligence Operating System.

---

# Hivemind

The shared engineering intelligence layer of Atlas.

The Hivemind maintains a unified understanding of projects by integrating engineering knowledge from every discipline.

It provides shared context, memory, relationships, constraints, and reasoning to all specialist agents.

The Hivemind is not an AI agent.

It is the collective engineering intelligence of Atlas.

---

# Specialist Agent

A specialized engineering system responsible for reasoning within a particular discipline.

Examples include Architecture, Structural Engineering, MEP, Construction, Sustainability, Planning, Code Compliance, and Simulation.

Specialist agents contribute knowledge to the Hivemind while consuming shared engineering intelligence.

---

# Agent Orchestrator

The system responsible for coordinating engineering workflows.

The Agent Orchestrator assigns tasks, manages collaboration between specialist agents, and communicates with the Hivemind.

It coordinates engineering work but does not own engineering knowledge.

---

# Continuous Engineering Reasoning

The continuous evaluation of engineering consequences as project information evolves.

Reasoning occurs proactively rather than waiting for explicit user requests.

---

# Engineering Memory

The persistent repository of engineering decisions, assumptions, reasoning, revisions, lessons learned, and validated knowledge accumulated throughout projects.

Engineering Memory enables knowledge to outlive individual projects.

---

# Knowledge Graph

A structured network representing engineering entities and their relationships.

The Knowledge Graph connects buildings, objects, systems, documents, regulations, decisions, people, and engineering concepts into a unified understanding.

---

# Semantic Object

An engineering object that possesses meaning beyond geometry.

A Semantic Object includes geometry, purpose, relationships, materials, engineering intent, constraints, history, and lifecycle information.

Examples include walls, beams, columns, slabs, doors, windows, equipment, spaces, and utilities.

---

# Building State

The complete engineering condition of a building at a specific moment.

Building State includes geometry, engineering systems, constraints, project context, reasoning outcomes, and historical information.

---

# Engineering Context

The complete set of engineering information required to correctly understand a decision.

Engineering Context may include building geometry, regulations, project goals, constraints, assumptions, materials, environmental conditions, and historical decisions.

---

# Project Context

The non-geometric information that defines a project's objectives, stakeholders, requirements, regulations, timelines, budget constraints, and engineering goals.

---

# Engineering Constraint

A condition that limits or influences engineering decisions.

Constraints may originate from physics, regulations, project requirements, structural systems, budgets, environmental conditions, or engineering standards.

---

# Engineering Rule

A validated engineering principle, regulation, code requirement, organizational policy, or project-specific requirement used during reasoning.

Rules should remain transparent and traceable.

---

# Semantic Relationship

A meaningful engineering connection between two or more entities.

Examples include structural support, adjacency, containment, dependency, service connections, ownership, accessibility, and construction sequencing.

---

# Continuous Learning

The controlled process through which Atlas expands its engineering intelligence using validated engineering knowledge.

Learning must preserve previously validated knowledge while incorporating new information.

---

# Building Lifecycle

The complete lifespan of a building.

The lifecycle includes planning, design, engineering, construction, commissioning, operation, maintenance, renovation, expansion, and eventual decommissioning.

---

# Engineering Recommendation

A proposed engineering action generated through continuous reasoning.

Recommendations should always be explainable, evidence-based, and traceable to engineering principles or validated knowledge.

---

# Explainability

The ability of Atlas to communicate the reasoning, evidence, assumptions, and engineering principles behind every recommendation or conclusion.

---

# Engineering Knowledge

Validated engineering information that can be reused across projects.

Engineering Knowledge includes principles, reasoning, standards, calculations, lessons learned, best practices, and verified engineering experience.

---

# Living Building

A building understood as a continuously evolving engineering system rather than a static collection of drawings or files.

A Living Building accumulates knowledge throughout its lifecycle.

---

# Engineering Intelligence Pipeline

The continuous flow through which Atlas receives information, understands context, reasons about engineering consequences, preserves knowledge, and improves future reasoning.

---

# Source of Truth

The authoritative location for a particular category of information.

Within Atlas, every engineering entity, rule, document, and architectural decision should have a clearly defined Source of Truth to eliminate ambiguity and inconsistency.

---

# Digital Twin

A continuously synchronized digital representation of a physical building that reflects its geometry, engineering systems, operational state, and accumulated engineering knowledge.

Within Atlas, a Digital Twin is enriched by semantic understanding, continuous reasoning, and engineering memory rather than serving as a purely geometric model.

---

# Built Environment

The complete collection of human-made physical environments, including buildings, infrastructure, industrial facilities, public spaces, utilities, and future engineered environments.

Atlas exists to understand and improve the built environment.
